My first day in taiwan

travel blog taiwan

Arrival

My journey to Taipei started where I got 2 hours earlier to make sure that I really was able to catch my flight. And oh boy, that was kinda a mistake? As it turned out, the previous flight didn’t even started yet, because there was a thunderstorm in Frankfurt. After nearly two hours, the airplane finally arrived. A lot of other passengers already missed there flight. An announcement told me that we would land 8 PM in Frankfurt and after looking again at my boarding pass, it told that 8:05 PM boarding for my flight to Singapore would start. Thankfully, another announcement told me that there was a bus waiting for the Singapore flight that would transfer all passengers directly to the airplane.

We landed safely in Frankfurt with the friendliest pilot and crew imaginable that again apologized for the delay. As I left the airplane, I saw someone with a big sing that read „Singapore“. He wanted to see my ticket and was a bit irritated. After a short discussion, I found out that there were multiple flights scheduled from Frankfurt. He took only the passengers for an asian airplane, but I booked the flight with Lufthansa. I hastily looked at my clock and saw that the boarding would begin in 5 minutes.

Before continuing, let’s please take a minute to rand about the Frankfurt airport together. It’s gigantic and at the same time extremely hard to navigate. There are the ports A, B, C, D, E and Z? I arrived at A and should somehow get to Z24. Additionally, there would be another passport check at the airport and the signs between the ports were not always that clearly. And if that was not enough, we didn’t dock directly at A but landed somewhere on the field and then needed to take a bus to the airport itself. 1 Knowing all that, I started to run. The bus was relatively quick, of course Z was nearer on A then A to B and with my European passport, the border control was pretty easy. On top of that, my connection waited an additional 10 minutes for people like me that had delays.

The flight between Frankfurt and Singapore was not really noteworthy. I watched „Good Luck, Have Fun, Don’t Die“ which on the one side was better than expected, on the other side my expectations were also really low. If your flight takes 13 hours, your expectations for any movies start to lower pretty quickly.

And then, after the longest flight in my life, I was finally there in Singapore. It was also my first time and Asia and I was absolutely hyped. Furthermore, I had 7 hours at the airport and needed at least something were I could waste my time on. I started walking seemingly randomly through the airport because I didn’t got a departure port yet for my last and final flight to Taipei. One of the wildest things I discovered were the mall. It had multiple lounges and massage chairs, delivery robots and even an aquarium. Even better, they had an TCG vending machine! I have never seen something like that before. To top that, there was an internal railway only to transfer passengers from one flight to the next. All in all: a pretty wonderful airport! I later found out that the Lufthansa app didn’t properly updated my flight information so I needed to go around the airport and find out myself where the hacky flight was departing. Thankfully, there were extremely large displays that showed all the current information about all arriving and departing planes. By the way, I rediscovered webtoon this weekend. Webtoon is an app were you can read manhwas, mangas and comics for free. It was once one of my great three biggest sources for wasting my free time procrastinating and still feeling good about it.

I got on my flight at around 0 PM and tried to get some sleep. They gifted me a small duvet which I could keep and in fact still use today2. During my flight I found out that Singapore and Taipei are both in the same time zone, which was kinda weird for me, as they were still 4 hours apart. Then I finally arrived at the airport safely and got through the border control. I needed to fill out an arrival card were I simply gave away all my private data. After an recommendation from an co-worker, I bought a SIM card at the airport3.

First challenge

Then I tried to get cash at the ATM because my landlord only accepted cash as an payment. Originally this was a bit fishy to me and all just shout out „tax evasion“, but according to the website, this was normal? So I started to take out the 42000 TWD (~1140€) that I needed for the first rent and the 2 month safety payment for any occurring damages. The ATM had English as a language option. I clicked through the first steps, then noticed that there was a limit of 20000 TWD and just choose that as the amount that I would withdraw. Then I got my receipt, my card — and nothing else. No 20000 TWD were anywhere to see. I started to panic. Did I just lost around 540€, just like that? Did I got scammed? I quickly checked my bank account and noticed that there weren’t any recent payments made through my card. So maybe it was just a small mistake?

I started to enter everything again at the ATM, selected 20000 TWD again, and got a receipt, my card but no cash back — again! How easy was it to trick me? Did I just lost 540€ again? Did I really do the same mistake twice? Then I tried to read the receipt. At the very top was clearly written: “withdrawal amount: 20000 TWD”. When I looked at the bottom, I noticed that there were transaction details ans something written in traditional Chinese. After translating it, I found out that this was an error message saying that I needed to select an lower amount, nothing was withdrawn at all and the transaction was cancelled. I tried again, this time with 5000 TWD and it finally worked. I later met other tourists from Germany who had an similar issue with the ATM and the transaction errors. Some even got a different error message each time they tried again. Some were able to withdraw a bit, but others gave up after receiving the third error message.

Because of these problems, I contacted my landlord and he said that it’s ok if I pay him later. After getting his reply, I gave up at the ATM and moved to the metro station to get from the airport to Taipei main station.

The metro system

One of the best things about Taiwan is definitely the metro system. The metro line between the airport and Taipei main station comes every 15 minutes, and the metros directly in Taipei around every 5 minutes. All metro lines have a unique color and are named after the first or first two letters of the color. Furthermore, all stations are numbered. The first station gets the number 1, the second station the number 2, and so on. If you know that you start at station 8 and must go to station 13, then you know that you must exit the metro in 5 stations. The name of every station is both written in latin and traditional chinese. To top that, on every second pillar there is a plate that shows the color, number and name of the station so you know at every stop where you are and how many stops there are to your destination. This makes traveling more convenient. They even grayed out the past stations at the info plate and highlighted all destinations of the line.

If you buy a ticket for the metro, you either get a coin if you bought a singe trip or a card if you have a subscription. At the entry of the metro, you either insert your coin or scan the card and when you exit it, you insert or scan it again. This way the metro operating company exactly know at which station you entered, at which station you left and how much they need to bill you.

On the first day I bought a single trip to Taipei and a 3-day pass for Taipei city itself. For the following weeks, I got an easy card together with a TPASS. The TPASS is a mega city pass with which you can take every transportation system inside the region. This pass costs 1200 TWD (~30€) and is valid for 30 days. It is kinda similar to the Deutschlandticket, but it’s only valid for the metropolitan area.
